E-WASTE AND ELECTRONICS DISPOSAL GUIDE

Electronics contain valuable materials (gold, copper, rare earth metals) but also hazardous substances (lead, mercury, cadmium).

NEVER place the following in general household waste:
- Mobile phones and smartphones
- Laptops and computers
- Tablets and e-readers
- Televisions and monitors
- Batteries of any kind (lithium-ion, alkaline, lead-acid)
- Printers, scanners, cables
- LED and CFL light bulbs
- Fridges, air conditioners (contain refrigerants)

WHERE TO TAKE E-WASTE:
- Manufacturer take-back schemes (Apple, Samsung, Dell all offer these)
- Certified e-waste recyclers (look for R2 or e-Stewards certification)
- Municipal hazardous waste collection events
- Retailer drop-off points (Best Buy, Staples, Currys)
- Local council waste facilities

BATTERIES specifically:
- Lithium-ion (phones, laptops): Most electronics stores have collection bins
- Alkaline (AA, AAA): Many supermarkets accept these
- Lead-acid (car batteries): Auto parts stores take these back

REPAIR BEFORE RECYCLING:
Many electronics can be repaired cheaply. Check iFixit.com for guides. Phone screens, laptop keyboards, and charging ports are commonly repairable.

Source: EU WEEE Directive, India E-Waste Rules 2022
