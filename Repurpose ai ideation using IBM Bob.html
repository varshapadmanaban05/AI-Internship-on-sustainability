Executive Summary

Every day, households, institutions, and businesses discard items that could be reused, repaired, repurposed, or donated — not because there is no alternative, but because people lack the knowledge, tools, and confidence to act on one. The result is accelerating landfill growth, unnecessary resource consumption, and a circular economy that fails to close the loop.

RePurpose AI is a conversational, AI-powered advisor that accepts a description (and optionally a photo) of an unwanted item and recommends the most appropriate circular pathway: reuse, repair, repurpose, donate, refurbish, recycle, or responsibly dispose. Recommendations are grounded in a curated knowledge base of sustainability, circular-economy, and waste-management guidelines using Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), ensuring advice is traceable, explainable, and reliable — not hallucinated.

The system is designed to be built as a realistic student prototype using IBM watsonx, open-source libraries, and a modest document corpus, while remaining architecturally extensible into a production-grade civic or enterprise tool.

Problem Statement

How Might We Statement How might we help individuals, institutions, and small businesses make informed circular-economy decisions about unwanted items — so that fewer items become waste, more resources are recovered, and the environmental cost of every discarded object is meaningfully reduced?

Context & Scale

  • The world generates over 2.01 billion tonnes of municipal solid waste annually (World Bank, 2022), with at least 33% not managed in an environmentally safe manner.
  • A significant portion of landfill content consists of items with viable reuse or recycling potential — furniture, electronics, packaging, textiles, and equipment.
  • The barrier is not always willingness; it is information asymmetry: people do not know what to do, who to contact, or whether repair or donation is even viable for a specific item.
  • Existing tools (recycling locators, general search) are fragmented, generic, and require the user to already know the right question to ask.

The Core Gap

There is no accessible, intelligent, item-specific advisor that can look at what someone has in front of them and say: "This item, in this condition, has the following viable second-life options — here is why, and here is how to act on each."

Shared Pain Points Across All Segments

  • Information gap: No single, item-aware source of guidance.
  • Decision paralysis: Too many vague options lead to default disposal.
  • Lack of explainability: Generic recycling advice doesn't say why an item qualifies.
  • No context sensitivity: Material composition, condition, and age all matter — but generic guides ignore them.
  • No action pathway: Advice without a next step (who to contact, what to search for) is unhelpful.
04

Why AI Is Genuinely Useful Here

This is not a problem that requires AI for novelty — it requires AI because the problem is structurally ill-suited to rule-based or lookup tools:

ChallengeWhy AI Adds Real Value
Item descriptions are unstructured, vague, and varied LLMs interpret natural language input regardless of how a user phrases it — no fixed taxonomy required
Pathway selection depends on multiple interacting attributes (material, condition, age, category) AI can reason across attribute combinations that would require enormous rule tables to cover manually
Knowledge base is large, multi-domain, and regularly updated RAG retrieves the most relevant guidance chunk at query time — no need to retrain the model as guidelines evolve
Image input is ambiguous (what material is this? how damaged is it?) Multimodal vision models can extract attributes from photos that text descriptions miss or get wrong
Users need explanations, not just answers LLMs naturally produce reasoning chains; RAG grounds them in source-cited content for credibility
Context varies by geography, item age, institutional constraints AI can incorporate context variables into the reasoning without hard-coding every scenario
Key Differentiator RePurpose AI does not replace human judgment — it augments it. The system's job is to surface the right knowledge at the right moment, explain its reasoning, and give users actionable next steps they would not easily find on their own.

Role of Multimodal AI

Why Image Input Matters

Users often cannot accurately describe the material, condition, or specific components of an item in text alone. A photo of a broken chair, a circuit board, or a packaging material contains information that a text description may omit or mischaracterize.

What the Vision Model Does

  • Item classification: Identify the type of object (e.g., "office chair," "CRT monitor," "blister packaging").
  • Condition assessment: Detect visible damage, wear, or structural integrity from the image.
  • Material inference: Infer likely material category (plastic, metal, wood, mixed) when not stated by the user.
  • Hazard flagging: Detect features that suggest hazardous materials (battery cells visible, liquid staining, labeling cues).

RAG Workflow & Knowledge Base Design

Why RAG Over Pure LLM Generation

A bare LLM may give plausible but unsupported sustainability advice. RAG ties every recommendation to a retrievable source, making the system auditable, accurate, and update-friendly without retraining.

End-to-End RAG Pipeline

1 — INGESTCurate & chunk documents
→
2 — EMBEDGenerate vector embeddings
→
3 — STOREIndex in vector database
→
4 — QUERYUser input processed & embedded
→
5 — RETRIEVETop-k semantically similar chunks
→
6 — AUGMENTChunks injected into LLM prompt
→
7 — GENERATECited, grounded recommendation

Knowledge Base: Document Corpus

CategoryDocument Types & SourcesRationale
Circular Economy Frameworks Ellen MacArthur Foundation guides, EU Circular Economy Action Plan summaries, WRAP (UK) circularity reports Define the core reuse hierarchy and circular-economy principles that anchor all recommendations
Waste Classification & Handling UNEP waste management guidelines, Basel Convention summaries (hazardous waste), national e-waste rules (India E-Waste Rules 2022, EU WEEE Directive) Ensure correct classification of hazardous vs non-hazardous items and legal disposal obligations
Material-Specific Recycling Guides Plastics recycling codes (1–7), metal recyclability tables, paper/cardboard guidelines, battery disposal (Li-ion, alkaline, lead-acid), glass types Inform material-level recommendations when item composition is known
Electronics & E-Waste iFixit repairability guides, manufacturer take-back scheme documentation, certified e-waste processor directories Electronics are the highest-risk and highest-value item category; specialist guidance is critical
Repurposing Inspiration Curated repurposing idea guides (furniture, textiles, packaging), upcycling NGO manuals Provides the "repurpose" pathway with concrete, illustrative examples
Donation & Refurbishment Networks Charity shop acceptance criteria (e.g., British Heart Foundation, Goodwill), NGO donation intake guides, furniture reuse network standards Informs whether an item meets donation-worthy condition thresholds
Responsible Disposal Household hazardous waste disposal guides, municipal bulky waste collection rules, MSDS sheets for common household chemicals Ensures the "disposal" pathway is safe and legally compliant

Chunking Strategy

  • Chunk size: ~300–500 tokens with 50-token overlap to preserve context across chunk boundaries.
  • Metadata tagging: each chunk tagged with item_category, material_type, pathway, and source_name to enable hybrid (semantic + filter) retrieval.
  • Top-k retrieval: retrieve 3–5 chunks per query; pass to LLM as numbered context blocks.

Embedding & Vector Store (Student-Feasible Options)

IBM watsonx.ai Embeddings sentence-transformers (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) ChromaDB (local, free) FAISS (in-memory, open source) Pinecone Free Tier

AI Decision Logic — Pathway Recommendation

The Reuse Hierarchy (Embedded in System Prompt)

The LLM is instructed to follow a prioritized pathway ladder. Higher pathways are preferred; the model must justify downgrading to a lower one.

PriorityPathwayCondition Trigger
1 — HighestReuse As-IsItem is functional and in acceptable condition for its original purpose
2RepairItem has specific, fixable damage; repair cost is proportionate to value; parts are available
3Repurpose / UpcycleOriginal function is obsolete but item material/form has alternative use
4Donate / RedistributeItem is functional but unwanted by current owner; meets condition thresholds for acceptance
5Refurbish (3rd Party)Item needs professional restoration before re-entry into use; refurbishment market exists
6RecycleItem cannot be reused but materials are recoverable; material type is recyclable
7 — Last ResortResponsible DisposalItem contains hazardous materials or is not recoverable; must follow safe disposal guidelines

Attribute Signals Used in Decision

The system prompt instructs the model to extract or infer these signals before selecting a pathway:

  • Item category (furniture, electronics, packaging, appliance, textile, etc.)
  • Condition (functional / minor damage / non-functional / hazardous)
  • Material composition (wood, plastic, metal, mixed, glass, fabric, battery)
  • Age / Generation (still relevant / outdated / obsolete)
  • Hazard indicator (contains battery, CRT, refrigerant, mercury, chemicals)
  • Repairability (modular / sealed / proprietary)

Prompt Architecture

System Prompt Pattern The LLM receives: (1) a system instruction defining the reuse hierarchy and output format, (2) retrieved RAG context chunks with source tags, (3) extracted item attributes (from text + optional image), and (4) the user's original query. The model must select a primary pathway, justify it using retrieved context, name the source, and suggest a concrete next action.

Output Format Per Recommendation

  • Recommended Pathway: e.g., "Repair → then Donate"
  • Reasoning: 2–3 sentences citing retrieved guideline
  • Source Citation: Document name and chunk reference
  • Next Steps: Specific, actionable suggestions (what to search, who to contact, what to check)
  • Confidence Note: Flag if the recommendation is based on inferred rather than confirmed attributes

Agentic Workflow

When Does an Agent Add Value?

A single-pass RAG query handles the core recommendation. An agent becomes valuable when the task requires multiple sequential steps, tool use, or dynamic information lookup that cannot be resolved in one LLM call.

Proposed Agent Architecture

A lightweight ReAct-style agent (Reason → Act → Observe → Repeat) with three tools:

ToolDescriptionWhen Invoked
KnowledgeRetriever Queries the RAG vector store for sustainability guidelines Always — primary grounding tool
ItemClassifier Calls vision model to extract attributes from an uploaded image When image is provided by user
HazardChecker Looks up item type against a hazardous-material list (e-waste, chemicals, batteries) When item category triggers potential hazard keywords

Agent Reasoning Flow

  1. Receive input — text description and/or image from user.
  2. Classify item — invoke ItemClassifier if image present; otherwise parse text attributes.
  3. Hazard check — if item is electronics, batteries, refrigerant-bearing, or chemical — invoke HazardChecker first.
  4. Retrieve context — invoke KnowledgeRetriever with enriched item description.
  5. Reason over context — LLM selects pathway from the reuse hierarchy, citing retrieved chunks.
  6. Generate response — structured recommendation with source citations and next steps.
  7. Clarification loop (optional) — if confidence is low, agent asks one targeted clarifying question before finalizing.
MVP Scope Note For the student prototype, implement a simplified agent with KnowledgeRetriever only (single tool). The ItemClassifier and HazardChecker can be stubbed as conditional logic branches or added in Phase 2. Full agent orchestration via LangChain or IBM watsonx Orchestrate is a natural enhancement.

Prototype Scope — MVP vs Future Enhancements

✦ Minimum Viable Prototype

  • Text-based item description input via chat UI
  • RAG pipeline over a curated 30–50 document corpus (PDFs + web scraped guides)
  • ChromaDB or FAISS as local vector store
  • IBM watsonx.ai (or OpenAI API) as the LLM for generation
  • Pathway recommendation from the 7-level reuse hierarchy
  • Source citations included in every response
  • Simple Gradio or Streamlit web interface
  • Basic confidence flagging in output
  • Hardcoded hazard keywords for e-waste flag
  • Static knowledge base (manually curated, not live-updated)

◈ Future Enhancements

  • Image upload with multimodal vision model (GPT-4o / LLaVA)
  • Full ReAct agent with multiple tools
  • Location-aware recommendations (local recycler/charity lookup)
  • Live knowledge base with scheduled re-ingestion
  • Barcode/QR scan for product identification
  • Carbon footprint estimate per pathway
  • User history and impact dashboard (items diverted from landfill)
  • Multilingual support
  • Integration with donation platform APIs (Freecycle, Too Good To Go)
  • Mobile app (React Native / Flutter)
  • Fine-tuned smaller model for offline use in low-connectivity contexts

Responsible AI Considerations

Transparency & Explainability

  • Every recommendation must include a source citation — the document name and the specific claim retrieved.
  • The reasoning chain (why this pathway was chosen over alternatives) must be shown to the user, not hidden inside the LLM response.
  • The system must clearly state when it is inferring attributes (e.g., "Based on your description, I am assuming this item is primarily plastic — please correct me if not").

Avoiding Unsupported Recommendations

  • The system prompt must prohibit the LLM from recommending disposal methods, chemical handling, or legal compliance pathways unless retrieved context supports them.
  • If no relevant context is retrieved (low similarity scores), the system should respond: "I don't have reliable guidance for this specific item — please consult your local waste authority."
  • Confidence thresholds should be applied: if top-k retrieval similarity falls below a threshold, flag the recommendation as low-confidence.

Safety

  • Hazardous items (e-waste, batteries, chemicals, asbestos-containing materials) must always trigger a hard safety warning before any other recommendation.
  • The system must never recommend home disposal of hazardous materials — always defer to certified disposal channels.
  • No personally identifiable information should be collected or stored; item descriptions are ephemeral per session.

Fairness & Accessibility

  • The knowledge base should include guidelines from multiple geographies and not assume a single regulatory context (e.g., do not default to EU rules for all users).
  • When geographic context is unknown, the system should note that specific local rules may vary and prompt the user to verify locally.
  • Interface should be accessible to non-expert users — avoid technical jargon in outputs.

Privacy

  • Images uploaded by users should not be stored beyond the active session and should not be sent to third-party services without disclosure.
  • For the MVP, use local vision models (LLaVA) or clearly disclose API usage if using cloud vision services.

Avoiding Over-Reliance

  • The system must include a disclaimer on all outputs: "This recommendation is advisory only. For hazardous materials, legal compliance obligations, or high-value decisions, consult a qualified professional or your local authority."
  • The system is a decision support tool, not a regulatory compliance engine.

Academic Evaluation Approach

For the internship demonstration, construct a test set of 25–30 item scenarios spanning diverse categories (electronics, furniture, packaging, textiles, chemicals). For each, manually define the expert-correct pathway and evaluate the system's output against it. This produces quantitative accuracy evidence suitable for a project report and presentation.

Conclusion

RePurpose AI addresses a genuine, measurable problem — the information gap that sends reusable, repairable, and recyclable items to landfill — using AI in a way that is both technically justified and practically feasible for a student internship.

The design is intentionally lean at MVP stage: a RAG pipeline, a curated knowledge base, a clear recommendation logic rooted in the circular-economy hierarchy, and a simple web interface. Every component is open-source or freely accessible for students. The architecture scales naturally into a production system with agent tooling, multimodal vision, and live knowledge updates.

Critically, the use of RAG ensures that the system's advice is traceable, explainable, and grounded in real sustainability guidelines — not generated from an LLM's opaque internal knowledge. This makes it trustworthy, auditable, and aligned with responsible AI principles.

Core Value Proposition RePurpose AI does not try to solve all of waste management. It solves one specific, high-frequency moment: the moment when someone holds an unwanted item and does not know what the right thing to do with it is. Making that moment better — more informed, more circular, more confident — is a meaningful and achievable contribution to SDG 12.
